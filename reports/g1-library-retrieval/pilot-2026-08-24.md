# G1 small-N pilot — 2026-08-24 (#200, phase 0 of #201)

Document-count sweep of the shipping retrieval defaults over 50 / 100 / 200-document
SciFact libraries, hybrid / vector / bm25, rerank off / on. This is a **run** of the
existing harness (`python/scripts/eval/g1_library_sweep.py`), not new machinery; the
harness's own report, manifest, per-cell arrays and rankings are published in
[`pilot-2026-08-24/g1-20260824T211215Z/`](pilot-2026-08-24/g1-20260824T211215Z/)
(copied from the git-ignored `runs/` output directory, per PROTOCOL.md §8.3). **The
published `manifest.json` and `report.md` are redacted**: `runtime.host`, every
`*_url`, `embedding_endpoints[_live]`, `argv`, `cwd` and the fixture/cache paths are
replaced with `<redacted>` (keys kept, so the schema is intact); the full-fidelity
originals live in the git-ignored `runs/` directory on the machine that ran the sweep.
Rankings are left as plain `.jsonl` rather than PROTOCOL §8.3's `.jsonl.zst` because no
reader in the repo (`g1_make_pool.py`, `aggregate_stats.py`) accepts `.zst` and
`zstandard` is not installed in the eval environment.

**Gate verdict (one sentence).** The gate as specified — *the best small-N config
within the CI of the large-corpus baseline on the same queries* — is **not answerable
from this run and is not failed**: the repository's baseline persisted aggregates only,
the pilot's queries are the 24/46/90 whose judged documents landed in the sample, and
the libraries are ~1.25-chunk/doc with an exact-scan dense leg, so 0.95 here vs 0.74
there is "small corpora are easy" (PROTOCOL §4.3), not a comparison; all within-run
evidence is in the benign direction — no cell scores below the shipping default by more
than δ = 0.02 with a CI excluding zero (the only CIs excluding zero are bm25-only being
*worse*), no configuration improves on the shipping default, and the harness's own
verdicts are INCONCLUSIVE at every stage, A/A gate passed, recommendation gate shut by
construction (A5) — so nothing in this pilot argues for a small-N branch of the
constants, and the distractor ladder remains the measurement that can answer the gate
as written.

### Grid

27 cells: 3 rungs × {hybrid, vector, bm25} × D ∈ {10, 100} × rerank {off, on}, minus the
D = 10 rerank-on cells. `build_grid` only keeps `rerank_candidates ≤ D`, and the default
`rerank_candidates` is 50, so rerank-on exists only at D = 100 (`_rr50`). D = 10 is the
shipping default (`top_k=5 × candidate_multiplier=2`); D = 100 is the harness's
designated-primary comparator (`top_k=5 × multiplier=20`). "Rerank on" therefore means
*the product's rerank-on path at its default pool size (50) over a D = 100 first stage*,
which at n50 is 50 of a 66-chunk union — the reranker sees most of the library.

## The four questions

**(a) Does RRF fusion still help vs dense-only at small N?** It does not hurt, and it
is the safer choice, but the pilot cannot show it *helps* against dense-only with any
confidence: hybrid − dense-only at the shipping depth is +0.026 [+0.000, +0.077] at
n50 (1 discriminating query of 24), −0.000 [−0.032, +0.032] at n100 and
+0.007 [−0.019, +0.034] at n200 — all CIs include zero, and on the 24 queries common
to every rung the point estimate stays positive (+0.026, +0.022, +0.057) without ever
excluding zero. The spec's worry that a starved BM25 leg would drag fusion down does
not materialise: at D=10 BM25 always returned the full 10 hits (`bm25_starved_rate`
0.000 in every D=10 cell; the leg only runs short at D=100, where 6–9 % of queries
have fewer than 100 term-sharing chunks), and RRF@k=60 over legs that overlap by only
3.4–4.2 of 10 still ranks at least as well as either leg alone. What *is* clear is that
hybrid beats **bm25-only** by a margin that grows with N and is significant at n200
(+0.082 [+0.036, +0.136], p = 0.001), so dropping the dense leg is the one change this
data rules out. Verdict: keep hybrid RRF as the default at small N; the dense-only
question stays open until the distractor ladder gives it more than 9 discriminating
queries.

**(b) Does rerank help or hurt at low candidate depth?** Neither, for the shipping
first stage — and it costs 4–12× the latency. Rerank on vs off over the same D=100
candidates is −0.026 [−0.073, +0.000] at n50, +0.013 [−0.023, +0.052] at n100 and
−0.001 [−0.034, +0.036] at n200 for hybrid, and indistinguishable from zero for
vector at every rung; against the shipping default it is −0.026 / +0.015 / −0.006, all
CIs spanning zero. The sole place the reranker clearly helps is on top of the weak
**bm25-only** first stage (+0.052 at n100, +0.061 [+0.019, +0.109] at n200), which is
the reranker repairing a bad first stage rather than adding to a good one; with the
hybrid first stage there is nothing left to repair (nDCG@10 ≈ 0.95, recall@20 ≈ 0.99).
Two mechanism notes: (i) the rerank-on cells converge to the same score whatever the
first stage was (0.958 for all three modes at n50, 0.963 for hybrid and vector at n100)
because a 50-candidate pool over a 66–251-chunk library *is* most of the library, so
the reranker's ranking is nearly first-stage-independent at these sizes; (ii) the
n50 hybrid dip (−0.026) is two queries (verified in `raw/*.rankings.jsonl`) where
the cross-encoder pushed the judged document down exactly one document rank
(1 → 2 and 2 → 3) from where RRF had it — consistent with the reranker being a
source of variance rather than lift on an already-saturated first stage. The harness does not emit a
D=10 rerank-on cell (`rerank_candidates=50 > D`), so "rerank over a 10-deep pool"
was not measured; the product's rerank-on default is the D=100-style pool.

**(c) Do the tuned constants need a small-N branch?** Not on this evidence. The
designated primary pair — shipping D=10 vs D=100, same `rrf_k`, no rerank — is
+0.000 at n50, +0.002 [−0.002, +0.009] at n100 and −0.006 [−0.026, +0.012] at n200:
depth does not move the metric, in either direction, at any rung. `rrf_k=60` was not
swept here (the harness default sweeps the shipping value only), but the per-leg
accounting shows the condition under which `rrf_k` could matter — a large asymmetry in
leg depth — did not occur: both legs return exactly D at D=10 and the union depth is a
stable 15.8–16.6. Nor is there a sign flip across rungs for any contrast (the
difference-in-differences the protocol §7.6 calls for has nothing to test on: every Δ
is inside ±δ = 0.02 at every rung except bm25-only, which is worse everywhere). If a
branch were ever warranted it would be *not to rerank* below some library size, not to
change `rrf_k` or the multiplier — but the perf constraint below already argues that
case on cost alone.

**(d) The gate: is the best small-N config within the CI of the baseline?** **Not
answerable as specified; not failed.** The literal test — *the best small-N cell within
the CI of the large-corpus baseline on the same queries* — cannot be run from this
data: the repository's large-corpus baseline (`scifact_chunk_eval`, `fixed_tok512`,
6,108 chunks: nDCG@10 0.698 [0.654, 0.742], recall@20 0.869, n=300) persisted only
aggregates, not per-query arrays; this run's queries are the nested 24/46/90 whose
judged documents all landed in the sample (a selection the baseline's 300 did not
undergo); and the libraries are 1.25-chunk/doc with an exact-scan dense leg. The
grid's 0.946–0.985 against the baseline's 0.742 upper bound is therefore
"small corpora are easy" (PROTOCOL §4.3) and must not be read as the gate comparison
in either direction. What the run *does* establish is that every piece of within-run
evidence points the benign way: no cell scores below the shipping default by more
than δ with a CI excluding zero except bm25-only (which is worse, not a candidate);
no cell improves on the shipping default; the designated primary is INCONCLUSIVE
(1 of 14 discriminating queries on the confirm split, −0.006 [−0.026, +0.012] on all
90); stage-1 flagged nothing, stage-2 is INCONCLUSIVE, the A/A gate passed, and the
recommendation gate is shut by construction (A5). Note that INCONCLUSIVE is not
EQUIVALENT (§7.5) — no equivalence test was passed, so the cells are *not shown* to be
tied; they are merely not shown to differ. The distractor ladder (protocol §4.3) is
the run that can answer the gate as written.

| vector D=100, rerank on (50) | 359 | 433 | **7.0×** | **10.3×** |
| bm25 D=10, rerank off | 4 | 15 | — | 0.3× |
| bm25 D=100, rerank off | 10 | 17 | — | 0.4× |
| bm25 D=100, rerank on (50) | 592 | 886 | **51.8×** | **21.1×** |

**Two measurement confounds, disclosed before the verdict.** (a) *The rerank score
cache is shared across cells.* `rerank_cache` is one dict created once per run
(`g1_library_sweep.py:2915`) and consulted per `(query, chunk)` before any sidecar
call (`:1394-1400`), and the grid order within each rung is bm25 `_d100_rr50` →
hybrid `_d100_rr50` → vector `_d100_rr50`. So the **bm25 rerank cell paid the cold
cross-encoder calls** and the hybrid/vector rerank cells only called the sidecar for
the pairs bm25's pool had not already scored (at n200, hybrid's union of 146 overlaps
bm25's 96 heavily). The 52× vs 3.7× spread is therefore largely a cache-order
artefact: **bm25's 592 / 886 ms is the cold figure; the hybrid and vector rerank-on
latencies are cache-assisted lower bounds.** (b) *No cell includes query embedding.*
Query vectors are precomputed once and served from `CachedQueryEmbedder` (protocol
§6.6, to keep the shared fleet out of the variance budget), whereas the product path
always embeds the query before the dense leg; and with 8 queries in flight against one
sidecar, the rerank p95 includes queueing. What *is* the product's path: the rerank
stage is the same `SidecarReranker` → `POST /rerank` the API uses, and the pool shape
(D=100 first stage, `rerank_candidates=50`, truncate to `top_k`) matches the shipping
config (`config.py:603-609`).

**Verdict, robust to both confounds: rerank-on does not stay within 2× of rerank-off
p95 in any mode.** The cache-*assisted* hybrid figure (518 vs 140 ms, 3.7×) already
fails the ≤ 2× constraint, and the cold figure can only be higher; the cold bm25
figure (886 vs 17 ms) fails by a wide margin; every rerank-on cell is 10–21× the
shipping default's 42 ms p95. The cost is the cross-encoder call, not the deeper
first stage: D=100 rerank-off adds only +17 ms p50 over D=10 for hybrid. Under the
protocol's own §5.5 constraint no rerank-on cell would be nominated at this rung
regardless of its quality, which — given (b) shows no quality gain on the hybrid first
stage — is the operationally relevant result of the pilot: **rerank-on by default
buys nothing measurable at ≤ 251 chunks and costs an order of magnitude in p95.**
A clean per-mode cold number needs a run with the rerank cache scoped per cell (or
cells run in isolation); it was not re-run here because the verdict does not depend on
it. Whether that changes when the first stage is genuinely noisy (a 36k+-chunk ladder
rung) is a question for the ladder. Per-leg means at n200: dense 22.7 ms and BM25
9.2 ms at D=10; dense 46.9 ms and BM25 18.7 ms at D=100 (rerank off).
