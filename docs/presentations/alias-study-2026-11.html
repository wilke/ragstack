Prereg §1

Two questions, two families

Family G · glossary

A question names a concept by its full term; the passage that answers it uses only a short form — a symbol, or an abbreviation the paper defines once, elsewhere.

Does attaching the paper's merged glossary to its chunks make that passage findable?

H1: merged > none on G records. H1b (only if H1 holds): the G gain exceeds the change on control (C) records.

Family N · growth

A real collection holds a paper with its references and citers, which reuse its vocabulary and short forms — often with different meanings.

Does adding citation neighbours cost more than adding the same number of random same-field papers?

H2: neighbour < random average. Plus Layer 2: the reranker default vs BM25 in a related-paper collection.

The study informs two serving decisions — ship the merged glossary? keep the reranker default? — it does not make them. Each family is tested one-sided at α = 0.05 on its own; no level passes between them (prereg §3.1).

Outcome · found@10

A record is found when its gold chunk is in the top 10, or another top-10 chunk, read alone, is judged to state the core of the answer.

A measure of reach, not answer quality. found@10-gold (gold chunk only, no judge) is printed beside every figure.

Design · the judge

What “judged” means

An LLM judge decides whether a chunk answers the question. The alternative, found@10-gold, only checks whether the one known source chunk was retrieved.

For each question the system retrieves its top 10 chunks. For any chunk that matters, the frozen judge reads the question and that one chunk, with the paper title and the chunk's glossary entries, and answers one question: does this passage, read on its own, state the core of what is asked? Any chunk can count: the same paper, another paper, or a passage other than the gold one. A question is found (judged) if at least one top-10 chunk is labelled answers.

  • Two Qwen models judge first: Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (n 5, T 0.7, thinking), and Qwen3.8-27B (n 1).
  • If they agree, that is the label; if they disagree, Gemini 4 Argon breaks the tie (1,230 of 24,559 pairs).
  • Claude Opus 5.5 re-labels a 5 % sample to watch for drift and never sets a label (next slide).
  • To save calls, a chunk is judged only when its verdict could change the outcome; a gold chunk in the top 10 already counts as found.

Gold vs judged

Gold: is the record's own source passage in the top 10? A fixed fact, no judge involved.

Judged: does any top-10 passage answer? Broader, because another passage can stand in for the gold one. That is why judged found@10 was already 591 of 600 G questions without a glossary, while the gold chunk was in the top 10 for 550 of them.

The cost of judging

It depends on the judge being right. On 100 spot-check pairs the owner's labels agreed with the judge's 0.872 of the time (re-weighted), below the prereg's 0.90 line. So every judged result carries judge validity below threshold (§13.5), with the gold figure beside it. The owner's tags on the 19 disagreements (exploratory) put every one on the partial-versus-full line, none a misreading.

Design · the judge

The drift reference: a monitor, not a judge

It checks that the judging pipeline stays the same over a long run. Across two stages, 24,559 pairs were judged on two Qwen endpoints and a gateway; an endpoint swapped to another model, a server setting or a gateway update could shift the judge without anyone noticing.

  • A seeded random 5 % of judged pairs (1,228 of 24,559) is also sent to Claude Opus 5.5, on alias-judge/1, the prompt of the earlier judge calibration: a fixed external yardstick.
  • The run tracks how often the final label agrees with Opus, overall and per corpus arm, as batches accumulate.
  • It never sets a label. If it could override labels it would be part of what it measures.

Rules (prereg §6.5, amended R29): reference lines 0.97 overall and 0.95 per arm, report-only; the one alarm that pauses the run is an arm gap above 0.05 once every arm has 100 Opus-answered pairs. An empty Opus answer (a known topic quirk) is re-issued once, then the pair is no reference: filtered, never a drift failure.

Drift reference vs the owner's spot check

Drift reference: is the judge consistent over time and across arms? One model against another.

Spot check: is the judge right? The owner against the judge. That one fell below its line (0.872 vs 0.90) and put the flag on every result.

Family N · the problem

Neighbours vs matched random papers

Why neighbours might hurt

OA-1000 is a sample of unrelated papers. A real collection holds each paper beside its references and citers: the same vocabulary, the same short forms, often meaning something else — about half of the abbreviations shared between papers collide (prereg §1).

More look-alike chunks can push the gold chunk out of the top 10.

Why neighbours might not

A neighbour is also more likely than a random paper to answer the question, and an answer from another paper counts as found. Judged H2 asks whether neighbours displace more than they add; the gold-only gate checks the displacement itself (§3.1).

Limitations

What this does not show

  • A judged reach metric. found@10 counts one chunk that, read alone, states the answer — not answer quality, not rank. The judge is below its validity threshold; gold-only, which needs no judge, agrees in direction on H1/H1b and points the hypothesised way on H2.
  • A single-chunk binary rubric. Two chunks that together answer, or one that answers in part, score as not found. On the owner's tags (exploratory, post hoc), all 19 owner–judge disagreements sit on that partial-versus-full line.
  • The ceiling. 98.5 % of G and 100 % of C records were found before any glossary. H1 rests on 6 records in 6 papers; a general upward shift on C was unobservable.
  • Selected splits in the control. Route, direction and stratum were read after the overall rate; the null was added after seeing it. Confirming the agreed-pair pattern needs a fresh, pre-registered test.
  • One corpus, one population. OA-1000 only; records LLM-drafted and LLM-verified, plus the owner's check of 40 (37 valid); questions use the glossary's primary term.
  • Two families, no joint error control. No sentence here combines “the glossary helps” with a growth claim.
