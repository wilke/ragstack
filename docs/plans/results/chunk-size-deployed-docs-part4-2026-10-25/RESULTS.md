## The answer

On questions that need two passages from a long page, with every arm given the same 4,096 tokens of
context:

| | hypothesis | verdict | Scout net | Qwen net |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H2 | semantic capped at 512 tokens is no worse than live (uncapped semantic) | **confirmed** | +15 (+4.0 pts) | +30 (+8.1 pts) |
| H4 | fixed 512-token chunks are no worse than live | **confirmed** | +4 (+1.1 pts) | +12 (+3.2 pts) |
| H3 | fixed 512 is no worse than fixed 1,024 | **confirmed** | −10 (−2.7 pts) | +5 (+1.3 pts) |
| H1 | fixed 512 gives more correct answers than fixed 128 | **not confirmed** | +19 (+5.1 pts) | +1 (+0.3 pts) |

n = 372 questions on 71 pages. Non-inferiority margin δ = 7.5 points. Holm over the four tests per
reader. "Confirmed" requires both readers.

In words:
- **Rebuilding the collection with a 512-token cap is safe.** The capped semantic arm is the best arm
  for both readers. Fixed 512 is no worse than live.
- **The pre-registered evidence does not support 512 over 128 at this budget.** The difference shows up
  when the budget is smaller in tokens, or when a fixed number of chunks is passed: see
  [secondary conditions](#secondary-conditions-and-sets).
- Two things qualify this:
  - the **grader validation** is pending;
  - the **reference-quote defect** found in addendum Q weakens H3 and, under one classifier, H4 on the
    clean questions.

## What was tested

The full design is in [prereg/PREREG-part4-confirmatory.md](prereg/PREREG-part4-confirmatory.md). In
brief:

- **Collection:** 336 public software-documentation pages (1,265 live chunks), read in place and
  read-only. The served pipeline is unchanged: BM25 + SFR dense → RRF k=60 → cut 50 →
  bge-reranker-v2-m3.
- **Arms:**
  - **L**, live: semantic, no token cap;
  - **SEM512**: the same semantic chunker, capped at 512 tokens;
  - **T128o0, T512o0, T1024o0**: fixed token windows, no overlap.
- **Questions:** all new, generated by Claude Opus 4.8 from fresh passages. GPT-5.6 Sol was the fallback
  when Opus returned nothing.
  - **Set M** is confirmatory: each answer needs two passages at least 1,500 characters apart on one of
    the 76 pages of at least 2,048 tokens.
  - **Set S** is a single-passage replication.
- **Readers** (the models that answer): Llama-4-Scout and Qwen3.8-27B, temperature 0. They are analysed
  separately and never pooled.
- **Conditions:**
  - **B4096** (primary): walk the reranked list and add whole chunks up to 4,096 tokens. Truncate the one
    that overflows at a sentence boundary.
  - **B2048:** the same walk at 2,048 tokens.
  - **k10:** the top 10 chunks, whatever their size.
- **Grading:**
  - **GPT-5.6 Sol** (primary) grades each answer correct, partial, incorrect or abstained against the
    question and its reference quotes.
  - **Claude Opus 4.8** grades every answer on a 25% subset of questions.
- **Test:**
  - a sign-flip permutation test with pages as the exchangeable units (10,000 flips);
  - non-inferiority by shifting each question's difference by +δ;
  - Holm over H1–H4 per reader;
  - a 90% page-cluster bootstrap CI on the net share;
  - "confirmed" only when significant under both readers.

These were reported outside the confirmatory family and are unadjusted. "Both" means p < .05 under both

| condition | H1 T512 − T128 | H2 SEM512 − L | H3 T512 − T1024 | H4 T512 − L |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M, B4096 (primary) | +19 / +1 | +15 / +30 | −10 / +5 | +4 / +12 |
| M, B2048 | +6 / +11, neither | **+33 / +44**, both | **+37 / +44**, both | **+32 / +41**, both |
| M, k10 (50% subset, n = 186) | **+33 / +29**, both | −7 / 0, reader-dependent | +9 / −13, reader-dependent | −7 / −6, neither |
| S, B4096 (replication, n = 363) | **+16 / +22**, both | +3 / 0, both NI | −1 / −1, both NI | −8 / +5, both NI |

(NI = non-inferior.)

What this shows:
- **At a small budget (B2048), large chunks lose.** Live and T1024 are the two worst arms, by 7 to 13
  points. Live's chunks reach 3,398 tokens and T1024's are 1,024. At 2,048 tokens, one or two large
  chunks fill the budget with text from a single place, and one of the two passages goes missing.
  Retrieval says the same: both passages are delivered for 143 of 372 questions on live and T1024,
  against 184–196 on the 512 and 128 arms.
- **At k10, the number of chunks matters more than their size.** T128 delivers about 1,270 tokens on
  average, against about 4,930 for T512. With a quarter of the text, it loses by 29–33 net. At k10, T512
  is also not shown non-inferior to live, which delivers about 5,820 tokens.
- **Set S is near its ceiling** (82–92% correct). Here T512 beats T128 under both readers. All three
  non-inferiority contrasts hold.

**Abstentions** (B4096, M) are where the arms differ most visibly:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scout | 38 | 30 | 46 | 39 | 29 |
| Qwen | 32 | 10 | 19 | 17 | 19 |

These are counts of answers that abstained. Qwen abstains 22 fewer times on SEM512 than on live.

## Addendum Q: are the reference quotes answer-bearing?

**EXPLORATORY.** The full design is in
[prereg/PREREG-part4-addendum-Q.md](prereg/PREREG-part4-addendum-Q.md).

**Why:** while checking the human grading page, the owner found an M question whose second reference
quote describes the question's **premise**, a step the question already mentions. It doesn't contain
anything the user asked for. The grader requires both parts of a two-part reference, so a full answer to
that question can only be graded *partial*.

The M prompt asks for a question "whose full answer needs both passages". Validation checked only that
each quote exists on the page and that the two lie far enough apart. Nothing checked that each quote
answers part of the question.

**Method:** each of the 744 (question, quote) pairs was classified on its own as one of:
- **answer:** the quote states part of what the user wants to know;
- **setup:** the quote only describes the situation the question already mentions;
- **neither:** the quote is unrelated to the question.

The primary classifier was Claude Opus 5.5 (temperature 0) and the secondary was GPT-5.6 Sol. Neither
had written the questions. A question is **clean** when both of its quotes are labelled answer.

**Prevalence (classifiers):**

| | Opus 5.5 | Sol |
|---|---|---|
| quotes labelled setup | 104 of 744 (14.0%) | 103 (13.8%) |
| quotes labelled neither | 2 | 4 |
| quotes with no label (Opus 5.5 returned nothing) | 15 | 0 |
| questions with at least one non-answer quote | 105 of 372 (28.2%) | 105 (28.2%) |
| clean questions | 259 | 267 |
| agreement between the two classifiers | | κ 0.84 (3-class), 0.85 (answer vs not), 729 pairs |

The second quote is more often setup than the first: 15.9% against 12.1% (Opus 5.5). By stratum, the
share of questions with a setup quote ranges from 10% to 34%.

**Sensitivity:** H1–H4 were recomputed, with exactly the part-4 procedure, on the clean questions only.
No answer was regraded. The full-set recomputation reproduces part 4's stored verdicts, p-values and
nets exactly.

| clean set | n | H1 | H2 | H3 | H4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opus 5.5 (primary) | 259 | unchanged | unchanged | unresolved on the clean set | unchanged |
| Sol | 267 | unchanged | unchanged | unresolved on the clean set | **changed** |
| both | 247 | unchanged | unchanged | unresolved on the clean set | unchanged |

How to read each column:
- **H2** stays confirmed on every clean set.
- **H3** loses Scout on every clean set (Scout −3.1 points, Holm p .12 on the primary clean set), so
  under the frozen reading rule it is *unresolved on the clean set*.
- **H4** on Sol's clean set: Scout's point estimate flips sign, from +1.1 to −1.5 points (Holm p .079).
  That counts as *changed*: the result becomes reader-dependent.

The confirmatory verdicts are not replaced, because the frozen addendum says they stand. The changes are
reported beside them.

## Threats to validity

1. **The reference quotes are imperfect, and the classifiers undercount the problem.**
   - The graders saw only the question, the short reference quotes (at most 300 characters each) and the
     answer. They did not see the pages or the passages the questions were written from. "Correct"
     therefore means the answer contains what the quotes state and does not contradict them. Claims in an
     answer beyond the quotes were not checked.
   - Two failure modes follow:
     - **setup quotes:** a quote states the premise rather than the answer, so a complete answer can be
       graded only partial;
     - **unrelated quotes:** a quote that has nothing to do with the question. The owner found 4 of 40,
       which no one had expected.
   - Both push correct answers down to partial. That is why the per-arm shares are lower bounds.
   - The classifiers put the defect at 28% of M questions. The owner rejected 12 of 40 quotes (30%,
     95% CI roughly 17–47%), against the classifiers' 14%. So the true rate is probably about twice the
     classifier estimate. Up to about half of the M questions could carry at least one bad quote. That
     figure is an extrapolation from 40 labels.
   - The **clean sets are only partly clean.** Of the quotes Opus 5.5 accepted, the owner rejected about
     1 in 6 (6 of 34). The clean-set sensitivity check filters only part of the defect. It shows which
     way the results move, not that they are free of the defect.
   - **What the defect can and cannot do:** it applies to every arm equally, because each contrast is
     paired within a question. A bad quote lowers the ceiling for all five arms on that question. It can
     dilute a real difference, but it cannot favour one chunking.
   - That limit holds only if the arms deliver the right content at the same rate on bad-quote questions.
     The clean-set results show this is close but not exact: H3 and H4 weaken there, while H2 does not.
   - **Bearing on the conclusions:** H2 is robust to the defect. H3 should be read as unconfirmed on clean
     questions. H4 is reader-dependent on one classifier's clean set. Absolute accuracy on M is not
     trustworthy.
2. **Grader validity is pending.**
   - The human validation in §5 is not done. If the humans agree with neither model (κ < 0.6 against
     both), H1–H4 become *grader-unvalidated*.
   - Retest κ (0.94) and cross-grader κ (0.83) establish **reliability**, not **validity**: two models can
     agree and both be wrong.
3. **Generated questions.**
   - Every question was written by a model (Opus 4.8) from a passage. No real user question was available
     (prereg set R is *absent*).
   - Real questions are vaguer and less tied to a single passage.
   - The results hold for questions whose answer is in the documentation. They do not cover questions
     about things the documentation doesn't say.
4. **One collection, one pipeline.**
   - The collection is 336 pages of one product's documentation. The pipeline is one retriever and
     reranker stack.
   - The finding about large chunks at small budgets depends on how big this collection's live chunks
     are: up to 3,398 tokens, because the semantic chunker was uncapped.
5. **The B4096 condition is near whole-page.**
   - The budget of 4,096 tokens is larger than 55 of the 76 M pages.
   - So under B4096 the arms differ mostly in how much text comes from other pages. B2048 and k10 are
     where chunk size bites, and they are secondary.
6. **Power for small losses is limited.** The prereg's power table gives per-reader power of only about
   0.38 to show non-inferiority at a true loss of 2 points (discordance .25). Confirmed non-inferiority
   here means "no loss as large as 7.5 points". It does not mean "no loss".
7. **SEM512 changed between exploration and confirmation** (D4-1). H2 is about the current cap, which is
   the one a rebuild would deploy.
8. **Test properties.** By the prereg's simulation, the superiority test is slightly conservative and
   the non-inferiority test slightly liberal at the margin: null rejection 0.050–0.058 at α .05.

## What this means for the collection

This recommendation is provisional until the grader validation is in.

- **Rebuild with a 512-token cap.** Capped semantic (SEM512) is never worse than live in any condition
  measured. It is much better at B2048 (+33 / +44), confirmed non-inferior at B4096, and robust on the
  clean questions. Fixed 512 is an acceptable alternative at B4096 (confirmed). At k10 it is not shown
  non-inferior to live.
- **Don't choose 1,024.** It is confirmed non-inferior to 512 at B4096 only narrowly, and that is
  unresolved on clean questions. At B2048 it is among the worst arms.
- **Don't choose 128 if the assistant passes a fixed number of chunks.** At k10, 128 loses heavily
  because it delivers a quarter of the text.
- **The defect behind this:** the live semantic chunker ignored `chunk_size` (#647, reproduced in the
  exploratory record). The library now caps semantic chunks at the embedding model's window (#802,
  #805). A per-collection cap of 512 is still not something the collection spec can express: see the
  backlog note in [LABBOOK.md](LABBOOK.md).

## Design of the next study

These are lessons for the cross-corpus study, from this run. They are a design input, not results.

1. **Build multi-passage gold differently.**
   - For each quote, the generator states which part of the question it answers, in its own words.
   - A quote that only restates the question's premise is rejected at generation time.
   - A second model and a human sample check each quote's role **before any retrieval or answering**. The
     check has a pre-registered acceptance threshold, for example at least 90% answer-bearing on a human
     sample of 50.
2. **Grade against the passage as well as the quote.**
   - The grader sees the reference passage (about 1,000 characters), not only the ≤ 300-character quote.
   - A second rubric item asks whether the answer makes claims the passage contradicts.
   - Quote-only grading is kept as a secondary reading, for continuity.
3. **Validate the grader before the confirmatory run, not after.** Run a human-graded calibration set
   (several graders, majority label) on pilot answers. Fix the grader and the rubric only after that.
4. **Use real questions where they exist.**
   - Real questions with locatable answers go in as a separate set with its own gold rule.
   - Unanswerable questions go in as a set of their own, so that abstention can be scored.
5. **Make the budget the primary axis.**
   - Here the result depends on the budget: at B4096 the arms converge, at B2048 large chunks lose, and at
     k10 small chunks lose.
   - The next study should treat delivered tokens as a factor (for example 1,024, 2,048, 4,096 and k10),
     with pages shorter than the budget reported separately.
6. **Pre-register how several graders combine,** including ties and per-grader reporting, before any
   grading. This run had to fix it afterwards (before any grade existed, but after the design was
   frozen).

The gateway runs need the gateway owner's approval and these safeguards:
- `fallback_model ""` on every request;
- at most 4 calls in flight;
- empty content counts as a failure;
- the run pauses at 5 failures in a row or more than 20 in total;
- an output-token cap, which pauses the run when reached.
